## The hurdle (what's priced in)
- Hurdle (D): EV $74.73B = 6.08x FY27 consensus revenue ($12.30B) and 11.1x FY27 adj. EBITDA ($6.73B). CoreWeave on the same ex-lease basis is 3.03x / 4.78x, so NBIS carries a ~+101% / ~+132% premium. At CRWV's EBITDA multiple, the stock would be ~$105/share (−57%). At CRWV's revenue multiple, ~$121 (−50%).
- Rule of thumb (D): at a constant 6.08x, each $1B of FY27 revenue is worth ~$6.1B of EV, or ~$19.7/share.
- Reverse DCF (E; 03_depreciation.xlsx v2 Reverse_DCF): the price requires a FY29+ EBITDA yield on gross BILLING GPU capital of ~48% at a 5-yr life (~52% at 4 yrs, ~45% at 6 yrs). That holds even if FY29–30 capex halves each year (EV −$48B / −$23B / −$6B; implied yield 51/47/44%). FY28 consensus equals ~$11.0M of revenue and ~$6.7M of EBITDA per billing gross MW on Capacity's base MW path.
- Cohort unit economics (E; 03_depreciation.xlsx v2 Cohorts; per gross MW; Capacity capex; 11% WACC). Project IRR at a 5-yr life, owned / leased: installed book ($12M, Hopper-era capex) 15% / 12%. Microsoft at $11.6M/gross MW (300 MW basis) −5% (leased), and 15% if the contract covers only 200 MW. Prepayments (55% of capex) lift equity IRRs to ~25–75% for the non-Microsoft cohorts. So the economics depend on new-deal pricing, not on the installed book or on Microsoft.
- Site model correction: Nebius is mostly leased, like CoreWeave (started leases $1,625M; $12.05B signed but not commenced). v1's 'owns more sites' framing is dropped. Leased sites need less capex (no shell) but pay rent of ~$2.0–2.5M per IT MW-year. At the Q2-deal ACV, leased-site project IRRs come out at or above owned-site IRRs.

## 2. Microsoft / Vineland: a physical genset ceiling turns slippage into contract-value risk
**Claim.** Vineland is a leased build-to-suit site running on 62 rented gas generators (1.98 MW each, 122.9 MW gross ceiling) and ~5 MW of grid power. The Bloom fuel cells and LNG tank are under stop-construction orders (Aug 6/10). Until Bloom is live, only ~4 of 9 tranches fit (~27 MW IT each, equal-size assumption), which caps the book near ~$1.4–1.5B/yr (~$350–390M/qtr) vs ~$3.5B/yr fully delivered. Microsoft can cancel a tranche after a missed delivery date plus grace period if no alternative capacity is offered, and late delivery triggers liquidated damages.

**Evidence.**
- Contract (R, 6-K Sep 8 2025 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465925088312/tm2525580d1_6k.htm; 20-F Note 1): 9 tranches, $17.39B owed regardless of use (take-or-pay), ~$6.96B upfront, billed through Oct-2031
- Physical evidence (E, Capacity): est. live IT load ~33–45 MW at end-Q2 and ~57–75 MW in Q3. The NJ regulator's 45-day permit deadline falls in early-to-mid Nov
- Capacity tranche track: T1–T4 within the genset ceiling. T5–T9 bill Q2–Q3'27 (base) or Q3'27–Q1'28 (bear)
- CONFLICT: the Aug 12 letter says all tranches are delivered, yet the Q2 top customer was ~$140M/qtr (24% × $582.3M) vs ~$193M for T1+T2 at an equal split and ~$870M fully delivered

**Quantification.** (E, 03_depreciation.xlsx v2 Microsoft; equal split $96.6M/qtr per tranche; haircut = SLA/service credits + per-tranche termination, not demand). FY27 total revenue vs base: bear −$0.58B (−$11/share); T5–T9 cancelled −$1.16B (−$23/share; −$69 vs consensus). PV of Microsoft revenue at 11%: $13.4B / $12.3B / $11.8B / $5.8B. **Probability / expected.** 35% bear timing (Bloom permit slips into 2027); 10% that T5–T9 are cancelled or re-cut → ≈ −$6/share incremental to leg 1 (E: 0.35×−11 + 0.10×−23); tail −$23 to −$69
